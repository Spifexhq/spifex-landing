Financial operations software sits between accounting, banking and the operating teams that move money. Choosing it is not the same as choosing an accounting system. The questions are different: how does the tool help you plan cash, control execution, reconcile evidence and report on ownership?

This buyer’s guide gives you a practical evaluation framework. Use it to define requirements before you compare vendors, and to avoid buying a tool that only solves one part of the lifecycle.

## Start with the operating problem

Do not start with a feature list. Start with the workflow that is failing:

- Cash forecasts are disconnected from actual payments.
- Approvals happen in chat and email.
- Bank reconciliation takes too long.
- Spend is not attributed to projects or departments.
- Reporting requires rebuilding the same data.
- AI access is requested without a clear permission model.

Write down the current process, who owns each step, and where the handoffs break. That becomes your requirements document.

## The modules to evaluate

A financial operations platform may include some or all of these modules. Decide which ones need to be in one system.

### Cash flow

- Planned inflows and outflows with dates, owners and categories
- Recurring templates and scenario planning
- Settlements, partial settlements and transfers
- Variance between planned and actual

### Banking and payments

- Bank account visibility
- Payment requests and approvals
- Batch payments and payee management
- Reconciliation with evidence

### Ledger and classification

- Chart of accounts and classification rules
- Journal entries and periods
- Audit-ready history

### Projects and departments

- Cost and revenue attribution
- Budgets and variance
- Ownership and approval boundaries

### Reporting and KPIs

- Dashboards for cash, settlements and exceptions
- Filtering by owner, project and department
- Exportable reports

### Governance and AI access

- Role-based permissions
- Audit trails
- Read-only AI connections with explicit scopes

## Evaluation criteria that matter

### 1. Does it connect plan to execution?

A cash flow plan is useful only if it connects to the payments and settlements that actually happen. Ask how the product links planned entries to bank movements and what happens when a payment is partial or late.

### 2. Can you explain every movement?

Look for ownership, category, project/department, evidence and change history. If a movement cannot be explained, the reporting is not trustworthy.

### 3. Are approvals enforceable?

Ask whether approval rules are enforced in the workflow or only documented in a policy. Test a payment above the threshold and a change to payee bank details.

### 4. How does reconciliation work?

Ask for a demo with real exceptions:

- Partial payment
- Combined bank transfer
- Missing reference
- FX difference
- Duplicate movement

A product that only matches perfect records is not enough.

### 5. What is the reporting model?

Check whether reports come from the same operational data or require a separate data warehouse. Ask how long it takes to produce a cash report after month-end.

### 6. What does implementation require?

Ask about data imports, chart-of-accounts setup, bank connections, user roles and migration. A short demo can hide a long implementation.

### 7. What is the security and AI model?

For AI access, ask:

- Is the connection read-only?
- What scope does it request?
- Who can authorize or revoke it?
- Are workspace boundaries enforced?
- Is there an audit trail?

## Questions to ask every vendor

- Show me a planned entry that becomes a bank settlement and a reconciled match.
- Show me an exception and how it is assigned and resolved.
- Can I restrict a user to one workspace or one approval threshold?
- What happens when a payee’s bank details change?
- How do you handle partial payments and transfers?
- Which reports are native, and which require exports?
- How is pricing structured as we add users and workspaces?
- What is included in the free plan, and what changes on paid plans?
- How do you support data import and onboarding?
- What happens to our data if we leave?

## Pricing questions

Compare total cost, not only the monthly price:

- Per workspace or per user?
- Limits on transactions, bank accounts, projects or members?
- Are approvals and audit history included?
- Is AI access an add-on?
- What does support cost?
- What is the contract length?

## A simple scorecard

| Criterion | Weight | What good looks like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Plan-to-execution link | High | Every payment can be traced to intent |
| Reconciliation | High | Exceptions have owners and evidence |
| Controls | High | Roles and thresholds are enforced |
| Reporting | Medium | KPIs come from operational data |
| Implementation | Medium | Clear import and onboarding path |
| Security / AI | High | Read-only scopes and revocable access |
| Price transparency | Medium | Limits and add-ons are explicit |
