Month-end close is not a date on the calendar. It is a process with dependencies, evidence and decisions. When the process is unclear, the close expands to fill the time available and still ends with unresolved items.

This checklist gives you a practical 12-step close that works for small and mid-sized finance teams. Adapt the sequence to your business, but keep the ownership and evidence.

## The 12-step month-end close checklist

### 1. Freeze the cutoff

Decide which transactions belong to the period and communicate the cutoff. Late invoices and unrecorded payments are the most common source of close rework.

Record:

- Last invoice date included
- Last payment date included
- Any approved exceptions

### 2. Confirm bank balances

Reconcile each bank account to the statement. Do not start with the accounting-system balance and work backward; start with the bank.

Identify:

- Cleared versus uncleared items
- Deposits in transit
- Outstanding payments
- Bank fees and interest not yet recorded

### 3. Reconcile receivables

Match customer receipts to invoices. Review:

- Partial payments
- Overpayments and credits
- Unapplied cash
- Overdue invoices with no collection activity

### 4. Reconcile payables

Match payments to supplier invoices and planned commitments. Check for:

- Duplicate invoices
- Unapproved invoices
- Missing evidence
- Vendor bank-detail changes

### 5. Review accruals and prepayments

Record expenses incurred but not invoiced, and release prepayments according to the period. Keep a schedule with owners and reversal dates.

### 6. Reconcile intercompany and transfers

Internal transfers should not be treated as income or expense. Match the outgoing and incoming sides, and resolve differences before reporting.

### 7. Verify payroll and taxes

Confirm payroll journal entries, benefits, commissions and tax liabilities. Compare the payroll provider report with the ledger.

### 8. Review inventory and cost of goods sold

For businesses with stock, reconcile quantities and valuation. Investigate:

- Negative stock
- Unexplained adjustments
- Freight and landed cost
- Shrinkage

### 9. Classify unplanned movements

Every bank movement should be linked to a planned entry or explicitly classified as unplanned. Unexplained items are not a closing detail; they are a control failure.

### 10. Produce the reporting pack

Prepare the reports leaders actually use:

- Cash position and runway
- Profit and loss versus budget
- Receivables and payables aging
- Project and department performance
- Key variances and exceptions

### 11. Review and sign off

Assign a reviewer who did not prepare the work. The review should focus on:

- Material variances
- Unusual journal entries
- Reconciliations with open items
- Changes in classification or estimates

### 12. Close the loop on exceptions

Record every unresolved item with an owner, next action and due date. A close is not complete because the date passed; it is complete when the exceptions are owned.

## A simple close calendar

| Day | Work |
| --- | --- |
| Day 1 | Cutoff, bank imports, initial reconciliation |
| Day 2–3 | Receivables, payables, accruals, payroll |
| Day 3–4 | Inventory, intercompany, tax review |
| Day 4–5 | Reporting pack, variance review, sign-off |
| Day 5–7 | Exception cleanup and retrospective |

For smaller businesses, compress the calendar but keep the sequence. The dependencies matter more than the number of days.

## Close metrics worth tracking

- **Days to close:** from period end to signed-off reporting.
- **Open reconciliation items at close:** should decline over time.
- **Post-close adjustments:** changes made after sign-off.
- **Exception resolution time:** how long unresolved items stay open.
- **Unplanned movement share:** how much activity escaped the plan.

## Common close failures

- Starting with the P&L instead of the bank.
- Allowing unclassified movements to remain open.
- Treating reconciliation as one person’s task.
- Changing the close checklist every month.
- Reporting numbers without variance explanations.
- Closing without a reviewer who is independent of the preparer.
